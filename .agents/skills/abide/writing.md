# Writing check

Recruiters skim. Every sentence should hold a fact about this candidate or this role.

## Keep

- The candidate's own words from the CV and profile where they are already plain.
- Concrete facts: names, numbers, tools, results, dates.
- Active voice with a person as the subject: "I built", "I cut", "I ran".
- Short paragraphs, plain sentences, ordinary punctuation.

## Cut

- **Inflated words:** delve, leverage, utilize, foster, robust, seamless, cutting-edge,
  passionate, thrilled, excited to, dynamic, synergy, spearhead, transformative, elevate,
  journey, landscape, testament, pivotal, unwavering.
- **Empty openers and closers:** "I am writing to express", "I hope this finds you well", "In
  conclusion", "Thank you for considering my application" as a whole paragraph.
- **Filler phrases:** it's worth noting, in today's world, when it comes to, in order to, at the
  end of the day.
- **Contrast tricks:** "not just X but Y", "It's not X. It's Y." State Y.
- **Generic claims:** a sentence that would fit any company or any candidate. Replace it with a
  fact about this role or cut it.
- **Fake depth:** trailing "-ing" clauses that interpret ("highlighting my commitment to
  quality"), dramatic fragments, a quotable final line.
- **Formatting:** bold in sentences, bullets in a cover letter, emoji, more than one em dash.

## Final read

1. Is every claim backed by a CV or profile fact, with its scope intact?
2. Does each paragraph mention this company or role, or a result that matters to it?
3. Would it sound normal said out loud to the hiring manager?
4. Are all form limits (characters, words) respected?
